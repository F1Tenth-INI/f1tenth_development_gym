Idea: train the LLD through stochastic substep rollouts

Goal
----
Train the LLD to model a shorter transition of h = 0.04 / n seconds instead
of directly modeling the complete 0.04-second control interval.

For each recorded transition (state_t, action_t, state_t+1):

1. Set predicted_state = state_t.
2. Keep action_t constant during the complete 0.04-second interval.
3. Repeat n times:
   - Build the LLD input from predicted_state and action_t.
   - Obtain the Gaussian distribution over the reduced dynamic delta.
   - Draw a differentiable sample with dist.rsample().
   - Reconstruct the next intermediate state using dt = 0.04 / n.
4. Train the final reconstructed state against the recorded state_t+1.

Why this could help
-------------------
Acceleration and orientation are reevaluated at every intermediate state.
This can represent nonlinear evolution within one control interval more
accurately than applying one endpoint delta. It resembles a stochastic neural
ODE or multiple-shooting transition model, rather than merely repeating the
existing 0.04-second transition.

Required changes and caveats
----------------------------
- The LLD output must explicitly represent an h-second delta. The current
  training targets and retrieved value-token differences represent changes over
  0.04 seconds. They need to be converted to rates or otherwise rescaled for h.
- Keep n fixed, or provide dt/h as an explicit model input.
- There are no observed intermediate states. Many intermediate trajectories can
  reach the same endpoint, so final-only supervision does not uniquely identify
  the substep dynamics.
- Recursive errors and gradients can accumulate. One-step, rate-consistency, or
  semigroup-consistency regularization may be useful.
- The terminal distribution is generally not Gaussian after repeatedly sampling
  Gaussian deltas and passing them through nonlinear state reconstruction.

Why naive sampled terminal MSE collapses variance
-------------------------------------------------
For a scalar Gaussian sample

    prediction = mu + sigma * epsilon,  epsilon ~ N(0, 1)

sampled MSE against target y has expectation

    E[(prediction - y)^2] = (mu - y)^2 + sigma^2.

The derivative with respect to sigma is 2*sigma, so the optimum is always
sigma = 0. In multiple dimensions:

    E[||mu + L*epsilon - y||^2] = ||mu - y||^2 + ||L||_F^2.

Thus terminal MSE treats every injected stochastic perturbation as extra error
and drives every substep covariance to the numerical minimum. Reparameterized
sampling allows this gradient to propagate; it does not make MSE a proper
distributional objective.

Possible probabilistic objective
--------------------------------
Generate several reparameterized terminal rollout samples and use a proper
sample-based scoring rule such as the energy score:

    ES = E[||X - y||] - 0.5 * E[||X - X'||].

The first term pulls terminal samples toward the observed endpoint. The second
term accounts for sample spread and prevents the automatic covariance collapse
of sampled MSE.

An alternative is deterministic recursive training with the Gaussian means,
combined with a separate likelihood objective for uncertainty. However, a
substep Gaussian NLL requires suitable substep targets, which are not present
in the current 0.04-second data.
